# FirstStep — Validation and Measurement Plan

**Status:** Proposed plan; no testing or measured results.

## Research questions
- Do first-time adult investors encounter confusing terms or unclear next steps?
- Would contextual education add value beyond existing Schwab resources?
- Does an optional guide improve comprehension without increasing pressure to trade?

## Suggested research sequence
1. **Discovery interviews:** 5–8 first-time investors to understand vocabulary, questions, and current behaviors.
2. **Prototype testing:** 5 moderated usability sessions on the three conceptual screens.
3. **Baseline:** establish existing onboarding and education engagement measures with permission.
4. **Compliance review:** evaluate educational language, risk disclosures, accessibility, and privacy.
5. **Controlled test:** only if earlier evidence supports the hypothesis and appropriate approvals are obtained.

## Proposed measures
| Measure | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Knowledge-check score change | Evaluate comprehension |
| Optional lesson start and completion | Evaluate engagement |
| Self-reported confidence | Understand perceived readiness |
| Onboarding completion | Check for unintended friction |
| Risk comprehension and confusion reports | Safety guardrails |
| Support contacts | Detect unresolved questions |

**Illustrative targets for discussion, not results:** +15 percentage points knowledge assessment, 60% completion of voluntarily started modules, +10 percentage points positive confidence responses. Targets should be revised after baseline research and stakeholder review.

## Decision criteria
Continue only if evidence shows improved understanding, no deterioration in risk comprehension, and no material increase in confusion or unwanted onboarding friction.

## Limitations
Seven public reviews have now been coded as an exploratory convenience sample, with source links in 01-Research/Customer_Review_Coding.xlsx. No interviews, prototype tests, or live product analytics have been conducted for this independent case study. Public app reviews are not representative of Gen Z customers.
